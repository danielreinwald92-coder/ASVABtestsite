120 words at the level Word Knowledge actually tests, each with a short plain-language definition

How Word Knowledge Works

The Word Knowledge (WK) section is a synonym test. Nearly every question gives you a word, either alone or in a short sentence, and asks which answer choice "most nearly means" the same thing. On the computer adaptive CAT-ASVAB you answer 15 scored questions in 9 minutes, and because WK feeds your Verbal Expression composite, it directly affects your AFQT score.

You can't memorize the dictionary, so study smart:

  • Learn roots and prefixes. Pieces like bene (good), mal (bad), dict (say), and anti (against) let you decode words you've never seen.
  • Use context. When the word appears in a sentence, read the whole sentence and ask what feeling or idea would fit in that spot.
  • Eliminate opposites first. Test writers love including an antonym among the choices. Cross out any choice that means the reverse, then any choice that's unrelated, and pick from what's left.

Work through the list below in short sessions, a band at a time. Saying each definition out loud in your own words beats silently rereading it.
